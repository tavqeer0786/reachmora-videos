# Chapter 1 — The Problem With One Big Black Friday

Think about how people actually shop for Christmas.

They don't always buy everything at once.

Someone might purchase a television in November, wait for a better toy deal, buy decorations a week later, and return to the store for groceries before Christmas.

Different needs appear at different times.

So if Walmart focuses all its attention on one shopping day, it risks missing customers who aren't ready to buy yet.

A second promotional event changes that.

It creates another moment when shoppers have a reason to check Walmart's prices and products.

And this isn't only about Black Friday.

Retailers now compete across early holiday promotions, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, and the weeks leading up to Christmas.

The shopping season has become a series of opportunities rather than one giant moment.

For Walmart, that creates a powerful possibility: instead of asking customers to make every decision on one day, it can remain visible while those decisions are still happening.

But there's a problem.

Customers can shop almost anywhere. They can open Amazon, visit Target, or compare prices across multiple websites.

So simply announcing another sale doesn't guarantee that shoppers will come back.

What does Walmart offer that can make a customer choose it again—even when competing retailers are offering deals too?
