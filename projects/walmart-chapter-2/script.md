# CHAPTER 2 — Walmart's Secret Weapon Isn't Just Price

The answer is convenience combined with scale.

Walmart has something that many online-only retailers cannot replicate in exactly the same way: a large physical store network working alongside its digital shopping business.

Consider a family preparing for the holidays.

They need a television, toys, snacks, groceries, batteries, decorations, and maybe a new kitchen appliance.

They could shop at several different retailers.

But Walmart wants to make it possible to handle many of those purchases in one shopping ecosystem.

That matters because customers don't always choose a retailer based on the price of one product.

They also consider how much time shopping will take, whether an item is available, how they can receive it, and whether they can complete several purchases in one trip.

Walmart's stores, website, app, and available pickup or delivery options give customers different ways to shop.

A customer might discover a product online, check availability, and choose a fulfillment option that fits their needs.

Someone else might visit a physical store and purchase several unrelated items during the same trip.

The important point is that Walmart can compete for more than one type of shopping need.

And that makes the second event more interesting.

A customer who didn't find what they wanted during the first event may have another reason to return.

But convenience explains why someone might shop at Walmart once.

It doesn't fully explain why they might keep coming back.

So what turns a single Walmart purchase into a repeated shopping habit?
